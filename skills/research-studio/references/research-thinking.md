# Research Thinking

## 1. Goal

Make the analysis sound and think like a careful researcher rather than a vague summarizer, consultant cliché machine, or trend-content writer.

---

## 2. Core principle

A good research answer does not just say something plausible.
It:
- defines the question clearly
- separates evidence from inference
- distinguishes stronger support from weaker support
- acknowledges uncertainty
- gives bounded conclusions
- offers rational next-step suggestions

---

## 3. What not to do

Do not write conclusions like:
- 值得关注
- 有一定潜力
- 未来可期
- 可以进一步观察
- 有机会跑出来

unless they are immediately followed by:
- why
- based on what evidence
- under what conditions
- what is still uncertain

Do not use long wording to hide thin thinking.
If a sentence could apply to almost any market, product, or trend, cut it or rewrite it.

---

## 4. Evidence discipline

Always separate:
- directly supported findings
- inferred but not fully proven judgments
- open questions

Every important finding should point to a concrete source situation:
- sourced by a clear identifiable source
- supported by more than one source
- weakly supported by limited material
- currently missing and waiting for evidence

Use language discipline:
- evidence-supported: “现有信息显示… / 多个来源一致指向…”
- inference: “更可能是… / 初步判断… / 当前更像是…”
- uncertainty: “目前证据不足以判断… / 这一点还需要补证据”

Do not invent evidence to make the analysis feel complete.
If a useful number, fact, or comparison is missing, mark it as 待补充.

---

## 5. Structure of a research-style answer

Prefer this order:
1. research question
2. core conclusion
3. main evidence and reasoning
4. uncertainty / counterpoints
5. rational recommendation
6. what to validate next

Do not jump from raw facts directly to an oversized conclusion.
Show the path of reasoning.

---

## 6. Rational recommendations

A good recommendation is:
- bounded
- actionable
- tied to the evidence
- aware of uncertainty

Examples:
- “现阶段更适合继续验证，而不是直接立项。”
- “如果继续推进，优先补用户付费意愿证据，而不是先扩功能。”
- “当前不确定性主要不在需求存在与否，而在供给侧差异化是否足够。”

Bad recommendations are vague and ceremonial.

---

## 7. Tone rule

Sound professional, analytical, and plain.
Do not sound like:
- a motivational speaker
- a trend blogger
- a consultant padding a slide deck
- a generic content summary tool

Precision is more important than smoothness.
