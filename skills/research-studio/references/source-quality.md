# Source Quality

## 1. Principle

Not all sources deserve equal weight.
When an important conclusion depends on external information, prefer stronger sources and note the difference between fact, estimate, and opinion.

Every important factual statement should have a traceable source situation.
If the source cannot be identified clearly enough, do not present the statement as solid evidence.

---

## 2. Stronger source types

Usually stronger:
- official company or platform documents
- official statistics or regulator releases
- direct product pages or public documentation
- reputable reporting with named sources and clear attribution
- first-hand statements or primary data

---

## 3. Weaker source types

Use with caution:
- SEO content farms
- low-substance reposts
- unattributed claims
- marketing-heavy pages
- vague commentary with no evidence
- rumor aggregation

---

## 4. Conflict handling

If sources conflict:
- do not force a neat conclusion
- identify what conflicts
- prefer the source with clearer attribution and better evidence
- downgrade confidence where needed

---

## 5. Confidence discipline

Separate:
- reasonably well supported findings
- plausible but weakly supported interpretations
- open questions that still need evidence

Do not present all findings with the same confidence level.

## 6. Evidence hierarchy

When possible, think in rough tiers:
- Tier 1: primary or official evidence
- Tier 2: reputable reporting or clearly attributed secondary synthesis
- Tier 3: weak commentary, reposts, unattributed summaries, SEO pages

Important conclusions should lean more on higher-tier evidence.
If the conclusion mostly rests on lower-tier material, reduce confidence and say so.
