# Workflow

## 1. Goal

Research a user-provided topic in a way that produces usable judgment rather than a loose collection of links.

---

## 2. Default process

### Step 0: front-load alignment
Before executing the main research workflow, identify the key requirement questions that affect the whole report.
Use `grilling.md` when the task has branching decisions, hidden assumptions, or a high risk of later rework.
Ask the necessary questions early in one batch when needed.
Do not leave major scope ambiguity unresolved and then interrupt the workflow halfway.

### Step 1: define the question
Before searching, restate the research target clearly:
- what is the exact topic?
- what does the user really want to know?
- is the user asking for orientation, evaluation, or a decision aid?

If the topic is broad, narrow it into a workable research question.

### Step 2: classify the topic
Classify it into the most useful type:
- market / industry
- product / project
- platform / content ecosystem
- concept / trend
- audience / demand
- monetization / business model

Then choose dimensions accordingly.

### Step 3: first-pass search
Before searching, build a small query plan.
Use `query-planning.md`.
Run an initial scan to establish:
- definition and scope
- major players or reference points
- current state
- obvious debate or uncertainty

### Step 4: dimension-based follow-up search
Search the important dimensions one by one rather than repeating the same generic query.
Use different query buckets for different dimensions.
Typical follow-up areas:
- demand / audience
- supply / competition
- monetization
- growth drivers
- risk / friction / constraints
- recent changes or trend shifts

### Step 5: cross-check
For any important claim:
- compare across multiple sources when possible
- prefer official, primary, or clearly attributable sources
- note disagreement instead of forcing false certainty

### Step 6: synthesize
Turn findings into a judgment:
- what seems true with relatively strong support?
- what is plausible but not yet well supported?
- what is missing?
- what matters most for the user's decision?
- what is evidence and what is inference?

### Step 7: bound the conclusion
Before reporting, pressure-test the conclusion:
- is the conclusion broader than the evidence allows?
- are there meaningful counterpoints or uncertainty?
- does the recommendation match the strength of the evidence?

### Step 8: report
Deliver a structured result.
Keep it compact when the user wants a quick read, but do not reduce a substantive research task into shallow bullet fluff.

---

## 3. Search discipline

Do not stop after one query.
At minimum perform:
1. initial scan
2. targeted follow-up by dimension
3. cross-check of major claims

If the topic is time-sensitive, prioritize fresher material.

---

## 4. Missing-information rule

If evidence is too thin:
- say what is missing
- say what can only be judged tentatively
- ask targeted follow-up questions when needed

Do not fake confidence to make the report look complete.

---

## 5. Scope control

Do not let the research sprawl endlessly.
Keep the research centered on the user's real question.
If a subtopic is interesting but not decision-relevant, mention it briefly or leave it out.
