# Grilling for Alignment

## 1. Goal

Interrogate the task definition hard enough that the final execution does not rest on hidden ambiguity, unspoken assumptions, or unresolved decision branches.

This is not casual clarification.
This is front-loaded alignment pressure.

---

## 2. Core principle

Do not stop at “roughly understood.”
Before execution, walk the important branches of the decision tree until the task is clear enough to complete in one uninterrupted flow.

The purpose of grilling is:
- to expose hidden assumptions
- to resolve branching choices
- to prevent halfway rework
- to surface the user's real success criteria

---

## 3. Scope rule

Grilling happens in the intake phase, before substantive execution.
Do not scatter these questions across the middle of the workflow unless a truly unforeseeable blocker appears.

---

## 4. How to grill well

A good grilling sequence:
1. identifies the top-level goal
2. identifies the most important branch decisions
3. resolves dependencies in the right order
4. keeps going until the task is execution-ready

Do not ask random questions.
Ask dependency-resolving questions.

Bad:
- anything else?
- do you have more details?
- any preferences?

Better:
- who is this report for?
- what decision should it help you make?
- do you want orientation, recommendation, or execution guidance?
- what would make the output unusable for you?
- do you want breadth, depth, or decision-readiness?

---

## 5. Question quality rule

Each question should do at least one of these:
- eliminate a major ambiguity
- choose between two materially different output paths
- reveal a hidden success criterion
- prevent a likely later rework

If a question does none of these, do not ask it.

---

## 6. One-at-a-time vs batched intake

Default rule for this skill:
- identify the full question set internally first
- present the user a compact batch of the truly necessary questions
- use `intake-question-batch.md` when you need a practical intake template
- once answered, proceed through the full execution flow without piecemeal interruption

If the user explicitly wants to be grilled interactively, you may ask them one at a time.
Otherwise prefer one strong intake batch, not drip-fed questioning.

---

## 7. Recommended answer rule

When helpful, do not only ask the question.
Also suggest the likely best answer or the default recommendation.
This makes alignment faster and prevents vague back-and-forth.

Pattern:
- Question
- Why it matters
- Recommended default if user has no preference

---

## 8. Stop condition

Stop grilling and start execution only when:
- the main objective is clear
- the output target is clear
- the success criteria are clear enough
- the biggest branch decisions have been resolved

Do not over-grill trivial details.
Do grill the decisions that would materially change the report.
